Lead follow-up & CRM

Build lead follow-up that respects the conversation

Good follow-up starts with ownership, context and a clear stop condition.

Jolpai editorial · 14 September 2026 · 2 min read

In this guide

Bring enquiries into one viewUse useful contextStop at the right timeMeasure meaningful progress

Bring enquiries into one view

A form submission, an email and a return customer may all refer to the same opportunity. Decide how to recognize duplicates before adding automation. Preserve the original message and assign a responsible person so the team can see who should respond next.

Use useful context

Draft responses from the customer’s actual request and approved service information. Do not invent familiarity or claim that someone reviewed a business when they have not. For a first implementation, let staff approve drafts. Their corrections reveal what the workflow needs to understand before more actions can run automatically.

Stop at the right time

Reminders should stop when the person replies, declines or asks not to be contacted. Define what happens when an opportunity changes owner. Check that updates from the inbox reach the CRM before another message is prepared. A follow-up system should support an ongoing conversation rather than create a separate sequence that ignores it.

Measure meaningful progress

Review response time, unassigned enquiries and whether leads reach a clear next step. Sending more messages is not a useful goal on its own. Start with inbound enquiries and keep the process understandable enough that a team member can pause or correct it without technical support.
